# Worked examples

## 1. The "quick" extra page

**Scope given:** 5-page website (Home, About, Services, Pricing, Contact), 2 revision rounds, $2,400, estimated 40 hours.
**Client:** "Looks great! Can you quickly add a Careers page before launch on Friday? Should be easy since the design is done."

**Verdict** — Out of scope. The agreement lists five named pages; Careers is a sixth deliverable.
**Recommended move** — Yes, with a price and a date.
**Reply to send**
> Happy to add a Careers page. It's a sixth page on top of the five we agreed, so I'd handle it as a small add-on: $360 (6 hours at the project rate of $60/h), delivered Tuesday. Friday's launch of the five agreed pages stays on track. Want me to go ahead?

*If they push back*
- "It's just one page" → "It is one page — design, copy layout, mobile check and testing. That's the six hours."
- "Can it be ready Friday?" → "Yes if Pricing moves to Monday. Which matters more for Friday?"

**Change order**
```
CHANGE ORDER #1 — Website
What is added: Careers page (1 template, up to 5 job listings, same design system)
What is not included: job application form or applicant tracking integration
Effort: 6 hours
Price: $360 ($60/h × 6 h — rate derived from $2,400 ÷ 40 h)
Delivery: Tuesday
Effect on current timeline: none
```

Gap exposed: the scope names pages but has no price for an extra one. Add "additional pages: $360 each".

## 2. Third round of revisions

**Scope given:** logo design, 2 revision rounds, $900. Both rounds used.
**Client:** "The CEO just saw it and wants to try it in green and with a different font."

**Verdict** — Out of scope. Two rounds were agreed and both are used; a new stakeholder is asking for a third.
**Recommended move** — Yes, as a paid round.
**Reply to send**
> Glad the CEO is involved. We've used the two revision rounds included, so this would be a third round: $150, back to you in two working days. To avoid a fourth, could the CEO send every change in one list? Shall I start?

*If they push back*
- "It's only a colour" → "Colour and font together change every file we deliver. It's a full round."
- "No budget" → "Understood. Then the approved version from round two is the final one, and I'll send the files today."

Gap exposed: the scope does not say who approves. Name one approver next time.

## 3. The honest one: it was the freelancer's mistake

**Scope given:** monthly newsletter, written and scheduled.
**Client:** "The link in this month's issue goes to last year's page. Can you fix and resend?"
**User adds:** "I'd like to bill the resend."

**Verdict** — In scope. The link was part of the deliverable and it was wrong.
**Recommended move** — Fix it today at no charge.
**Reply to send**
> You're right, that link was mine to check. Corrected and resending to the people who clicked, within the hour. I've added a link check to my send routine.
